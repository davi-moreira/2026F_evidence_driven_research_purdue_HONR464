44  Station 5: Develop the pathway

WarningUnder development

This station is part of a book in active development and has not yet been through the author’s review. Content may change as the review advances.

What you can defend when you leave. Commit to the research pathway your question and your licence actually support, and say what that pathway can and cannot establish.

44.1 What this station produces

Research Contract v1: objective by target and reach by data strategy by warrant, with the pathway declared and its limits written before any result exists.

This is a versioned checkpoint, not a pass. You date it, number it, and attach the reason for the version. When later evidence changes it, you write the next version rather than editing the last one, because the sequence of changes is itself part of your research record.

44.2 Choosing your pathway

Five pathways run through this part of the book, and your question plus what you are allowed to do picks one. Read the row that fits, then read one that does not, so you can say why yours is right.

If your question asks and you your pathway is
how much, how many, or how distributed something is observe the world as you find it observational descriptive
whether one thing causes another observe the world as you find it observational causal
how much of a property exists, measured cleanly assign conditions yourself to measure it experimental descriptive
whether an intervention works assign conditions yourself experimental causal
what will happen to cases nobody has seen fit and honestly score a forecast prediction

Two cautions. Assigning conditions does not make your question causal: a randomized study can be measuring a property the world already has. And a causal question whose data cannot identify it stays causal — it becomes “causal, currently unidentified”, never quietly descriptive.

If your project combines pieces of several rows, read the complex-designs lesson as an overlay after you have declared your primary pathway.

44.4 The practice

  1. Use the route guide above to choose your pathway, then read that lesson and at least one that does not fit, so you can say why yours does.
  2. {‘Declare your pathway and write the crossing that licenses it’: ‘what makes your comparison mean what you claim.’}
  3. {‘Write the limits’: ‘what this pathway cannot establish no matter how clean the execution.’}
  4. Update your claim boundary to match the pathway, and mark the change as a new Contract version.

44.5 The four rails, here

Four concerns cross every station in this book. At this station they take these specific forms.

  • Ethics, permissions, and data exposure. A change of pathway can change your permission status; recheck rather than assume.
  • Evidence, provenance, and reproducibility. Name the prior work that used this pathway on a comparable question.
  • AI activity, verification, and human decisions. Ask an assistant to argue for a different pathway, then answer the argument yourself.
  • Uncertainty, claim boundary, and revision history. Different pathways carry different sources of uncertainty; name yours here.

44.6 What sends you back

Realized data that will not support the declared pathway sends you back here before analysis, not after.
