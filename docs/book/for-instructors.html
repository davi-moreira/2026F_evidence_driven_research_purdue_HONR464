Appendix B — For Instructors

EDR|AI is a self-contained manual: a student working alone needs nothing beyond the chapters and their companion Colab notebooks. This appendix is for the other reader — the instructor who wants to teach with the book. It presents the companion course the book grew out of and makes all of its materials freely available.

B.1 Two artifacts, one loop

The book and the course are different artifacts, and keeping them distinct is what lets each travel on its own.

  • The book is the manual. Every chapter ends with an It is your turn section, and every chapter has its own companion Colab notebook (the badge at the top of the chapter) where a reader runs the chapter’s code and completes that section. Worked in order, the 37 sections chain into a complete research project; the book stands on its own as a self-study path.
  • The course wraps around the book. Its weekly lab notebooks are classroom artifacts derived from the book’s chapters and shaped for live, 50-minute flipped sessions; its readings are the book’s chapters (required) with matching RDSS chapters recommended; and its milestones collect the chapters’ It is your turn sections, so each graded step of the student’s semester project is a piece of the book’s chain.

When the book changes, the course material is resynchronized from it: the English edition of the book is the source of truth for everything downstream.

B.2 The companion course

The reference implementation is HONR 46400 — Evidence-Driven Research, a semester-long Honors seminar at Purdue University (three 50-minute meetings a week, individual research projects, a public poster conference, and an oral evidence defense). What students learn is the working structure of contemporary scientific research: turning curiosity into an answerable question, designing a study whose claims are honest by construction, producing and verifying evidence along the main pathways, stating uncertainty like a professional, and defending a claim in public, with AI agents doing the legwork inside that structure. Everything student-facing is open:

  • Course website: davi-moreira.github.io/2026F_evidence_driven_research_purdue_HONR464 (syllabus, schedule, material)
  • Repository: github.com/davi-moreira/2026F_evidence_driven_research_purdue_HONR464
  • Course datasets, one bundle: honr46400_datasets.zip

The weekly rhythm: Monday and Wednesday lectures are led by a student — the day’s Student Research Lead, whose system (handbook, preparation template, rubric, question bank) lives with the course materials in project/srl/ — as Socratic investigations built on the week’s lab notebook; the AI does the legwork live, and every claim it produces is verified before it survives. Friday is a studio: a short retrieval quiz, a research stand-up, and a long sprint on the week’s milestone, worked with the student’s AI assistant (reviewer roles from an AI bench — at Purdue, GenAI Studio — are encouraged) and submitted the same day.

B.3 Milestones: grading the book’s chain

The project runs through sixteen milestones, M0–M15, one per week, from a first curiosity map to a defended final research chapter. Each milestone brief names its book anchor: the chapters whose It is your turn sections the student completes and submits that week. Grading the milestone is therefore grading a stretch of the book’s chain, worked on the student’s own project. The briefs are open in the repository: _research_project/2026Fall/.

B.5 Adopting or adapting

The book is deliberately institution-agnostic, and the course material above is open: take the whole architecture, one lab, or nothing but the reading sequence. Instructor-side material — solution notebooks and per-meeting session guides — lives in a private repository linked from the Instructor tab of the course website; write to the author for access (About the Author). If you teach with EDR|AI, the author would genuinely like to hear how it went.

B.6 Grading rubrics — the “It is your turn” sections

One rubric per chapter, derived from the chapter’s numbered steps. The same rubric appears in the chapter’s companion notebook, so what you grade is exactly what the student saw. Scoring, for every rubric: 0 missing · 1 attempted · 2 complete, project-specific, and verified.

B.6.1 Ch. 1 — AI Is Your Arm, Not Your Brain

Each row scores 0 (missing), 1 (attempted but incomplete, generic, or unverified), or 2 (complete, specific to your own project, and verified where a check applies). 12 points total.

# Criterion 0–2
Step 1 Open a blank spreadsheet or document and title it AI Research Ledger
Step 2 Write down three genuine curiosities from your own life, work, or field
Step 3 Under each one, write the single fact you would need to know before you could take that curiosity seriously as a project
Step 4 Pick the curiosity you cannot stop thinking about, and ask an AI tool for three published sources that speak to that fact
Step 5 Log the exchange in your AI Research Ledger, and verify at least one output with a named method from the Verification Guide
+ Craft and verification record: AI use logged in your AI Research Ledger, claims stated with their uncertainty, and each key claim verified with a named method
