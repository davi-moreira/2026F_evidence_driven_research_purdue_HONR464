## Purpose

A poster is not a shorter paper. It is a **ninety-second read** delivered to
someone standing up, who did not come for you, and who will leave if the first
thing they see does not tell them why they should stay. Everything the artifact
knows has to survive that compression without growing.

The draft exists so that four readers can find, in a room where it is still
cheap, the wrong inference your board invites. A defect found on Wednesday costs
you an evening. The same defect found at the Expo costs you the claim.

Two rules govern this milestone, and both are checked:

1. **Every number on the board traces to the cell that produced it.** A number
   with no run behind it is a fabricated number, whatever route it took onto the
   poster.
2. **The uncertainty rides in the same eye-span as the claim it qualifies.** A
   headline in the reading position and its interval in a bottom-right footnote
   are not read together, which means they are not communicated together.

## Components

### 1. The storyboard, built before the software

Six blocks on paper, in the order a visitor's eye travels them, with the
headline placed in a compass position you can name and defend. Draw it before
you open the template. A poster laid out inside the software takes the shape the
software offers; a poster laid out on paper takes the shape the argument needs.

### 2. The draft itself

Built on the course template, sized to the URC print spec, and complete in the
sense that every block exists. Rough is fine. Missing is not: a reviewer cannot
find the defect in a block you did not draw.

Check it once at print scale rather than screen scale. Text that is comfortable
on a laptop is frequently unreadable from two metres, and two metres is where
your reader will be standing.

### 3. The traceability sweep

Go number by number. Beside each one, write the notebook and the cell. Circle
anything you cannot locate in a minute. Those circles are your blocking list,
and they are the reason this sweep happens now rather than on Sunday.

### 4. The reach check and the Poster Critic

Two AI-assisted steps, both interrogated rather than accepted:

- **The reach check.** Paste your headline sentence and its interval; ask for
  every word that reaches further than the interval licenses, with no rewriting.
  Then confirm or dismiss each flag against your own claim–evidence table.
- **The Poster Critic.** Run the GenAI Studio Poster Critic on the draft and ask
  it to state what a visitor would conclude from the board alone, without being
  told your claim. What comes back is a reading, not a compliment, and a reading
  is what you can act on.

Both go in the ledger with the verification method named.

## Three Traps

1. **The board built inside the template.** You open the template first, and the
   layout it offers becomes the argument you make. Draw the six blocks on paper
   first; the software should serve the order, not set it.
2. **The number that came from the write-up.** You copy a figure from your M10
   prose rather than from the run, and the prose was rounded. The sweep catches
   it now; the Expo would not.
3. **The compliment mistaken for a review.** You ask a tool whether the poster
   looks good and it says yes. Ask instead what a visitor would conclude from the
   board alone — that answer is usable, and it is often not the claim you made.
