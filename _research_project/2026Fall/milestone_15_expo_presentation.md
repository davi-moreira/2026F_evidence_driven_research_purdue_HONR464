## About the Research Project

Your semester project is **individual by default**: one researcher, one question,
carried from curiosity to a defended, reproducible claim. With instructor
approval, and only if at least three active projects remain and the required
peer-evaluation assignments are feasible, you may instead complete the same
chain as a group. The same five Final Project components apply
in either mode. Unless this brief marks a requirement as individual, an approved
group submits one shared artifact naming every member, and its shared rubric
rows receive a common score. Each member's AI Research Ledger and any other
requirement marked individual are scored per member, so recorded milestone
scores can differ only on those rows. Every member remains responsible for the
complete work and their own defense. Peer Evaluation remains a true peer
rating: group members rate every teammate; an individual-project researcher
is rated by two instructor-assigned project peers; nobody rates themselves. See
[Final Project — Grading and Project Modes](final_project_grading_and_project_modes.md).
It runs through milestones
**M1–M17**, peaks publicly at the **Purdue Fall Undergraduate Research Expo
poster session (Tuesday, November 17 — required)**, and closes with the
reproducible package and cold run in December. Milestone weights live in
[Final Project — Grading and Project Modes](final_project_grading_and_project_modes.md); instructions and
rubrics live one page per milestone, like this one.

This is the public test. On **Tuesday, November 17**, you stand next to your
printed poster at the **Purdue Fall Undergraduate Research Expo** and present
your research to people who did not come for you. **Presenting is required.**
This milestone confirms that you did, with a photo of you in front of your
poster.

As the Expo may not coincide with our regular class time, tell your other
instructors about it well in advance. If a scheduling conflict comes up, write to
me as soon as you know.

## At the Expo

- **Arrive early and set up.** Be at your poster for your **entire assigned
  session**, with the board ready before visitors arrive.
- **Present with your M12 plan.** Use the pitch that fits the time each visitor
  gives you, point at the block that carries each claim, and keep the boundary
  sentence at every length.
- **Answer from the evidence.** When a question reaches past what your design can
  settle, say so, and offer the smaller true claim you prepared.
- **Write down the questions you are asked.** After each visitor leaves, jot one
  line: the gist of the question and who asked it (a student, a faculty member, a
  judge). Note anything you hear more than once. These notes are the raw material
  for your conference reflection (M16), and a note written in the moment is worth
  far more than a memory reconstructed days later.
- **Visit other posters** when your session allows. Seeing how other researchers
  present is part of what the Expo teaches.

## How It Is Graded

**M15 milestone score (completion).** One of the seventeen scores averaged inside
**Milestone Deliverables**:

| Outcome | Score |
|---|---:|
| A photo of you in front of your poster at the Expo, submitted on time | 100 |
| No photo submitted, or a photo that does not show you with your poster at the Expo | 0 |

**Late:** −10 points per day, up to 3 days; not accepted after (documented
emergencies handled individually per Purdue policy).

**Your live presentation** is scored separately, at the Expo, with the rubric
below. That score supplies 30% of the Final Project's **Poster Presentation at the
Purdue Undergraduate Research Conference** component (12% of your course grade);
the other 70% is the poster-rubric score of your M11 final poster. For an approved
group, the poster part is shared, and every member receives their own
live-presentation score. The live presentation has no revision window. A required
presentation missed without an approved emergency arrangement scores 0 on both
this milestone and the live presentation.

## Live Expo presentation rubric (100 points)

| Criterion | Exemplary | Proficient | Developing | Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bounded explanation and research accuracy** (30) | Explains the question, design, finding, and exact claim boundary accurately at both the short and full pitch lengths; no spoken upgrade beyond the evidence (27–30) | Accurate and bounded with one thin transition or imprecise phrase (21–26) | Omits a load-bearing design choice or lets one spoken claim exceed the poster (13–20) | Cannot state the research chain accurately, or presents association as cause or sample as population (0–12) |
| **Evidence and traceability under questions** (25) | Answers from the poster, notebook, or ledger; identifies where a number came from and distinguishes what was checked from what remains assumed (23–25) | Answers are evidence-grounded; one trace requires prompting (18–22) | Relies on memory or assertion for multiple questions (11–17) | Cannot trace a central number or invents support under pressure (0–10) |
| **Uncertainty, limitations, and concession** (20) | States uncertainty with the claim, names the most threatening limitation, and concedes a smaller true claim precisely when warranted (18–20) | Limit and uncertainty are accurate but one is delayed or loosely phrased (14–17) | Treats uncertainty as a footnote or resists a warranted concession (8–13) | Presents certainty the evidence does not support or refuses an evidence-required correction (0–7) |
| **Audience responsiveness** (15) | Adapts the explanation to the visitor, listens before answering, and handles difficult questions directly without hiding behind jargon or AI (14–15) | Clear and responsive with one rehearsed or indirect answer (11–13) | Mostly recites the pitch regardless of the question (6–10) | Dismisses questions, blames a tool, or cannot engage the visitor (0–5) |
| **Professional readiness** (10) | Present and set up for the whole assigned session; poster displayed and ready; engaged with visitors throughout (9–10) | Present for the session; one readiness detail thin (7–8) | Late, partly absent, or set up after visitors arrived (4–6) | Required presentation not completed without an approved emergency arrangement (0–3) |

## Three Traps

1. **The photo left for the end.** The room clears, the posters come down, and
   the photo never happens. Take it in the first minutes of your session.
2. **The generous yes.** A visitor asks whether the finding would hold elsewhere,
   and "broadly, yes" gives away everything you spent the semester bounding. Use
   the concession you prepared at M12.
3. **The questions you meant to write down.** By Friday you remember the pleasant
   conversations and forget the hard question you could not answer. Write one
   line after every visitor.
