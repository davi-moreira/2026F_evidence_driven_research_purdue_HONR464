# Peer Evaluation — Instrument and Logistics

**Brightspace item:** *Final Project: Peer Evaluation*

**Due:** see the assignment on Brightspace; it is collected at the end of the
course.

Peer Evaluation is one of the Final Project's five components. It is worth
**20% of the Final Project, or 10% of the course grade**. It produces an
individual score in both project modes.

> **Peer Evaluation is not Peer Review.** Peer Evaluation is a confidential
> rating by classmates who observed your contribution and follow-through. Peer
> Review is your structured criticism of other projects. They are separate
> components, and there is no self-rating in either project mode.

## Who evaluates whom

The instructor records two lists for every student early enough for meaningful
observation: the **received-rating set** (the classmates who rate you) and the
**submission set** (the classmates you must rate). Every student has at least
one required evaluation to submit, so the completion fraction in the scoring
rule always has a defined denominator.

| Project mode | Received-rating set | Submission set |
|---|---|---|
| **Individual project (default)** | **Two instructor-assigned project peers** who observe your preparation, contribution, communication, dependability, and reciprocal support during scheduled studios and checks. | The project peer or peers the instructor assigns you to observe and rate—normally two. This need not be reciprocal with the two peers who rate you. |
| **Approved group project** | **Every other member of your project group.** | Every teammate, plus any individual-project researcher the instructor assigns you to observe. |

With five students, the instructor approves at most one group of two or three,
and only when at least three active projects or posters will remain. Every
student submits every evaluation in their submission set. Receiving a rating
and being assigned to rate someone need not
be reciprocal. You never rate yourself. A classmate who has not observed your
work cannot be added only to create a rating.

## Confidentiality and effect on grades

- Submit the form individually through Brightspace.
- Only the instructor and, if assigned, the TA see identifiable responses.
  Aggregated feedback may be returned without naming evaluators.
- The score is calculated **per student** from ratings that student receives.
  Students attached to the same project can therefore earn different scores.
- Failure to submit affects the non-submitter's own submission points, never the
  score of the classmates they were assigned to evaluate.
- A missing rating is never entered as zero for its intended recipient. The
  instructor follows up with the assigned evaluator and calculates from any
  valid ratings received. If evaluator non-submission still leaves no valid
  rating, the received-rating portion is set to the neutral **80 points**
  (equivalent to a mean rating of 3) so the intended recipient is not penalized.
  No substitute evaluator is added after the observation period.

## The instrument

For **each** person in your submission set, provide five numerical ratings and
two short responses.

### Numerical ratings (1 to 5)

| Dimension | What to rate for an individual-project peer | What to rate for a group-project teammate |
|---|---|---|
| **Commitment** | Prepared for scheduled studios and checks, stayed engaged, and followed through across the project. | Showed up, stayed engaged, and remained involved through the full shared project rather than only at deadlines. |
| **Technical contribution** | Brought substantive research work to the sessions you observed and used evidence rather than appearances of progress. | Contributed substantively to the design, evidence, analysis, verification, writing, or other research work. |
| **Communication** | Kept assigned peers informed, raised problems early, and made their work understandable enough to examine. | Responded within the group's agreed time, kept teammates informed, and raised problems early. |
| **Dependability** | Completed the preparation or follow-up promised for scheduled peer checks. | Delivered what they committed to, by when they committed to it. |
| **Fairness of work distribution** | Gave fair reciprocal support in the assigned peer relationship rather than coasting on or taking over the other person's checking and feedback. | Took a fair share of the shared work, neither coasting nor quietly absorbing everyone else's tasks. |

Scale: **1 = far below expectations · 2 = below · 3 = met expectations ·
4 = above · 5 = far above.**

A rating of 3 is the normal outcome for someone who met the commitment. Use 1
or 5 only when you can identify specific observed evidence in the comments.

### Short responses for each person

1. **One specific strength.** Name something concrete this person did well.
2. **One specific area for improvement.** Name something concrete they could
   do differently.

Comments such as "great teammate" or "did nothing" are not usable evidence.
Name the observed action, the setting, and its effect on the work.

## Scoring rule

Let \(\bar r\) be the mean of all valid 1–5 ratings the student **receives**
across the five dimensions and all evaluators. Let \(c\) be the number of
complete evaluations the student submits divided by the nonzero number in their
submission set; a complete evaluation contains all five ratings and both usable
comments. The 0–100 Peer Evaluation score is

\[
0.80 \times \min\left(100,\;100\times\frac{\bar r}{3}\right)
+ 20c.
\]

- Meeting expectations means \(\bar r=3\). With a complete submission
  (\(c=1\)), that yields **80 + 20 = 100**.
- Ratings above 3 confirm full received-rating credit but do not create bonus
  credit above 100; the comments still document the stronger contribution.
- Ratings below 3 reduce the received-rating portion. For example,
  \(\bar r=2.5\) with a complete submission yields **86.67**.
- Completing all required evaluations with usable comments earns the full
  **20 submission points**. Completing none earns **0**. A partial submission
  earns the same fraction of the 20 points as the fraction completed.
- If assigned-evaluator non-submission leaves no valid received rating after
  instructor follow-up, use \(\bar r=3\) for this calculation. This neutral
  fallback affects only the intended recipient's received-rating portion; the
  evaluator who failed to submit still loses their own submission points.

The instructor may moderate ratings that appear strategically inflated or
deflated only when documented evidence supports the adjustment. The moderation
record must identify the evidence and the adjusted received-rating mean; it
does not change another student's score merely because that evaluator failed
to submit.
