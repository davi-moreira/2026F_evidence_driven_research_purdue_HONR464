## Purpose

A visitor stops at your poster on Nov 17. Your first 30 seconds decide whether
they stay. You prepare three spoken versions, the 30-second, 90-second, and
2-minute pitches M12 asks for, that say exactly what the poster says, compressed
but never inflated. The cardinal rule: the mouth may compress the evidence, it
may never upgrade the claim.

## Materials

- Your final poster, the print file you submitted at M11 (content internalized, headline claim sayable cold).
- Your inquiry declaration §5–6 (the boundary you must not cross aloud).
- Your uncertainty line and the limitation you'll speak (feeds the boundary sentence and the ULN moment in your M12 plan).
- A partner and a timer for the delivery rounds.

## The three architectures

Count the words in every script and show the count. The ceiling stands in for
the clock, and the visitor sets the clock, not you.

**30-second pitch (≤ 75 words)** — the job is to earn the next ninety seconds.

```
1. HOOK          your title and question, in one sentence a stranger
                 outside your field cares about
2. CLAIM         your headline, spoken at its true size
3. BOUNDARY      one sentence: what this does NOT show ("this is an
                 association / a sample / a forecast, so it cannot say…")
4. INVITATION    "the surprising part is on the left — want the longer version?"
```

**90-second pitch (≤ 200 words)** — the 30-second spine, plus how you got the
number and what it is.

```
1. HOOK + CLAIM        (as above, tightened)
2. DESIGN + METHOD     how you got the number, in one spoken sentence
3. KEY RESULT          the number with its uncertainty, hand on the figure
4. BOUNDARY            the same sentence, in the same words
5. WHY IT MATTERS      one sentence
```

**2-minute pitch (≤ 300 words)** — the full walk: the evidence and the honest
bound.

```
1. HOOK + CLAIM        (as above, tightened)
2. DESIGN + METHOD     (as above)
3. EVIDENCE            strongest block first — what it shows, in one spoken
                       sentence each; the weakest kept, honestly labeled
4. INTERPRETATION      what the result means, at its true size
5. ULN MOMENT          uncertainty → limitation → next step (the ULN move);
                       the limitation carries the boundary sentence
6. INVITATION          where the finding could matter, then hand the question
                       back: "what would you probe first?"
```

Each longer version **adds** blocks to the same spine; it does not restate a
longer, looser claim. The boundary sentence appears at all three lengths, in the
same words. Every evidence sentence is a compression of a poster block, checked
against it word for word.

## The compression-never-inflation rule

Compression drops detail; inflation adds certainty. Saying "in our sample, most
respondents…" as "people generally…" is inflation — the excited-mouth upgrade
from "is associated with" to "causes" is the classic one. Your boundary sentence
is the antidote: rehearse it until it is automatic, so the claim can never
outrun it live.

## Procedure (50 minutes)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Poster-phase debrief + what Nov 17 actually looks like (logistics). |
| 5–13 | Mini-lesson: the three architectures, demonstrated live — once well, once inflated. You spot the exact word that upgraded the claim. |
| 13–28 | **Scripting studio:** write your 30-second pitch on the template; mark your boundary sentence; count the words. |
| 28–42 | **Delivery round:** deliver the 30-second pitch to a partner **twice** (swap roles between). The partner flags every spoken upgrade. |
| 42–48 | Revise the script from the flags. |
| 48–50 | Exit ticket (your boundary sentence, exactly as you'll say it). |

Homework: draft the 90-second and 2-minute pitches. All three scripts and the
poster-walk map join the rest of your M12 presentation plan (the audience
analysis and Wednesday's question strategy), due Wednesday, November 11,
11:59 PM.

## Roles

- **Presenter:** delivers cold, from the script, twice — the first attempt is officially a draft.
- **Partner (upgrade-flagger):** listen only for claims that outrun the poster; raise a hand the instant a word inflates, and name the word.

## Quality checklist (before submitting M12)

- ☐ 30-second script has all four moves; the boundary sentence is marked; ≤ 75 words, count shown.
- ☐ 90-second (≤ 200 words) and 2-minute (≤ 300 words) versions add the design, the result with its uncertainty, and the ULN moment to the same spine; counts shown.
- ☐ The boundary sentence appears at all three lengths, in the same words.
- ☐ Every spoken claim matches the poster's written boundary — no upgrade.
- ☐ A poster-walk map names the block your hand points at for each length.
- ☐ Delivered aloud at least twice; partner flags addressed.

## Common failure modes

1. **The excited-mouth upgrade.** "Associated with" becomes "causes" when the adrenaline hits. Drill the boundary sentence until it's reflex.
2. **Inflating instead of compressing.** Dropping the frame word ("in our sample") is not shortening — it's overclaiming. Keep the qualifier, cut the aside.
3. **Three scripts, three claim-sizes.** If the 30-second version reaches for a stronger verb, or the 2-minute version spends its extra time on certainty, the claim inflated. Same edge at every length: add evidence, not certainty.
4. **Reading, not delivering.** A script read verbatim loses the visitor. Rehearse to the point of eye contact.
5. **No boundary in the hook.** A 30-second pitch with no "what this can't show" invites the overclaim the longer versions can't take back.
