# RDSS coverage matrix — what EDR|AI adopts, adapts, omits, and adds (D43)

**Purpose.** The preface used to say EDR|AI "translates" RDSS (Blair,
Coppock & Humphreys, *Research Design in the Social Sciences*, Princeton
UP; book.declaredesign.org). The acceptance record
(`BOOK_DESIGN_ACCEPTANCE.md`) flags that as an overclaim: EDR|AI's scope
is quantitative empirical research directed with AI, while RDSS spans the
wider design space. D43 replaces the claim with "draws its design
framework from" and this matrix records the actual relationship, chapter
by chapter, against the verified RDSS inventory in `READING_MAP.md`.

**Dispositions.** *adopted* = the idea is taken over largely as RDSS
states it · *adapted* = the idea is reworked for the undergraduate,
AI-directed, code-light setting · *omitted* = deliberately out of
EDR|AI's scope · *EDR|AI-original* = no RDSS counterpart.

**EDR|AI-original (no RDSS counterpart):** the AI-direction spine (ch. 1–4:
arm-not-brain, research director, SDIIVDD, ownership; the AI Research
Ledger); prediction treated as its own answer objective (ch. 16); the AI
practice chapters (ch. 21–22 programmer/assistant, ch. 25 adversarial
reviewer, ch. 26 false confidence, ch. 33 disclosure, ch. 37–39 agent
management and the release portfolio); the twelve-Studio practice grain
with versioned Milestone chapters, practice kits, and the four rails; the
Verification Guide; venue contracts (Studio 10); the D43 opening move.

**Status.** Dispositions are the maintainers' reading of the verified
RDSS chapter inventory (`READING_MAP.md`, checked against the published
site); rows are conceptual mappings, not claims of equivalence. Davi's
positioning review may adjust individual rows; the preface links the
repository rather than restating the table.
