## The questions

| ID | Type | Question |
|---|---|---|
| `student` | single line | Purdue username |
| `conference_learned` | essay | What you learned at the Expo beyond your own poster; one thing another presenter did that you would borrow, and why |
| `visitors_count` | single choice | Roughly how many people stopped (0–5 / 6–10 / 11–20 / more than 20) |
| `visitor_types` | multiple choice | Who stopped (students, faculty from far fields, people who know the topic, judges, family and friends, someone else) |
| `audience_describe` | essay | Two or three sentences on who the audience was and what they wanted from the poster |
| `questions_asked` | essay | Up to five questions remembered most clearly, from the Expo notes, each marked as aimed at communication or at claims |
| `recurring` | essay | The question or kind of question that came up more than once, or "none recurred" |
| `target` | single choice | Whether the questions mostly aimed at communication, at claims, both, or too few to tell |
| `hardest` | essay | The hardest question, how it was answered, and what a good answer would need |
| `did_well` | essay | One thing the presentation did well, judged against the questions |
| `differently` | essay | One thing to do differently |
| `notes` | single choice | Whether the questions were written down at the Expo |

The `target` item carries the distinction the M16 plan depends on: a question
aimed at **communication** is fixed in how the work is presented, while a question
aimed at the **claims** may change the research itself.
